0:00–1:30 · Inclusive audit. Hi, this is my Part B digital education portfolio. I started by going back to my Part A resource, Can AI Translate This?, which I designed for Year 7 and 8 Chinese students. I wanted to check what students would actually need to do as they moved through the page. The original already had keyboard-friendly choices and a clear privacy reminder, so I kept those features. But I found that I had given students Chinese examples without a pinyin option. There was also quite a lot of reading before the activity, and the final question prompted a reflection without giving students a way to save it.

On my Inclusive Practice page, you can see the audit log and the two versions together. I used CAST's three UDL pillars to organise the changes. For engagement, students can now choose between two familiar situations. For representation, I added a pinyin switch and divided the information into smaller steps. For action and expression, students can write a translation, name their evidence, and keep a copy for feedback. I would still want to test these choices with students before claiming that the changes work for everyone.

1:30–3:30 · Learning task. The new task is called The Translation Evidence Lab. I kept the focus on Chinese expressions that change with context, but students now have to check a translation claim. I'll open the student page and choose the classmate example. The original sentence asks for help sending a photo. The AI-style suggestion sounds reasonable at first. Students compare that wording with the situation and a language note, then decide how they would write it in English. The second case is about thanking a teacher, and it brings in the expression 辛苦了.

The output samples are written by me and clearly labelled as simulations. Students do not need an AI account or a live AI tool. This helps me keep the task consistent, and it avoids unnecessary data collection. I chose the information literacy theme because I want students to ask where a translation claim came from, and what kind of evidence would help them check it. The Chinese curriculum link is AC9LC8EC05: interpreting and conveying meaning in familiar contexts. TPACK also helped me align the tool with the language objective. The page displays the sources and collects the student's reasoning. I remain responsible for checking that reasoning and discussing better alternatives with the class.

3:30–4:45 · Unit learning outcomes. For ULO1, students practise a privacy check, and I have made it clear which material is teacher-generated and which is being treated as an AI-style output. For ULO2, technology supports a real Chinese learning decision. It lets students compare information, revisit it, and explain a revision. For ULO3, the inclusive audit leads to specific changes in access and response options, including pinyin, step-by-step structure, and a printable answer. My next check would be to watch students use the page and look at whether their reasons actually refer to the context and evidence. That would tell me what to change next.
