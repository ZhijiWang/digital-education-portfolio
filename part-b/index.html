Professional statement

My approach to digital teaching

In my secondary classroom, I want students to question an answer from a digital tool, even when it looks finished. In Chinese, that might mean checking a translation. In Photography, it might mean looking again at an edit; in Games Coding, testing suggested code. I want to hear how students made their decisions, because a convincing final product does not tell me much about what they understood.

I speak several languages and still hesitate over expressions such as 辛苦了. “You have worked hard” can be a translation, but after a teacher stays back to help, I would probably say “Thanks for staying back.” Kramsch (1993) writes about language in its cultural context. It matters to me that students look at what someone is doing with an expression before choosing the English.

I put the Chinese message, suggested English and classroom context together in the Translation Evidence Lab. Students can check a phrase in a dictionary and return to the exchange while writing their own version. TPACK connects the choice of technology to what students are learning and how they learn it (Mishra & Koehler, 2006). For this lesson, students need to compare the wording with what happened in the conversation, so the page keeps both in view.

Teacher agency starts with my choice of examples, tools and what counts as evidence in class. I write and label the AI-style translations, and I decide which outside sources students can use. Student agency comes when they question a suggestion and choose how to translate it. Their explanations give me something to read before planning the next lesson.

The original page also needed changes so students could use it independently. I explain those changes and my use of CAST’s (2024) UDL guidelines in Inclusive Practice.

The Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools (Australian Government Department of Education, 2023) addresses privacy and responsible AI use in schools. My examples are invented and clearly labelled, and students need no AI account. I would keep names and private conversations out of external tools and collect their answers through a school-approved platform.
