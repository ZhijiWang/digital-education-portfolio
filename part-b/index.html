Professional statement

My approach to digital teaching

In my Chinese classroom, I want students to notice what happens between a sentence and the situation in which someone says it. An AI translator may produce fluent English for 辛苦了, but a student still needs to decide whether the speaker is thanking someone, showing sympathy, or acknowledging effort. That decision depends on the relationship and the moment.

I use digital activities when they give students something worth examining. Mishra and Koehler’s (2006) TPACK framework helps me plan from the learning problem: Chinese language knowledge, the discussion students need, and a technology that lets them compare and revise. In The Translation Evidence Lab, students inspect a suggested translation, look at the original message and context, and write a more suitable version. The website organises the evidence. I read the reasons they give.

My school placement has reinforced the value of that approach. In Year 8 photography, changing a camera setting matters when a student can explain the effect on a portrait. In Games Coding, a moving sprite matters when students test what happens and repair it. I want the same habit of testing and explaining in Languages.

Students also need room to participate differently. CAST’s (2024) Universal Design for Learning guidelines encourage varied ways to engage, access information, and show understanding. I now offer optional pinyin, shorter steps, two classroom situations, and a structured response. These supports are available to everyone without asking a student to disclose a learning need.

I keep digital safety visible in each lesson. The Australian Framework for Generative AI in Schools (Australian Government Department of Education, 2025) informs how I explain privacy, transparency and responsibility. I provide teacher-created examples, use school-approved platforms when external tools are needed, and keep names and private messages away from AI services. Students can question an output, use other sources, and make their own translation choices. I remain responsible for the task, the feedback, and the conditions in which the technology is used.
