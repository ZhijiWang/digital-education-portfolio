Can AI Translate This?

When more than one answer looks right

For
Year 7–8 Chinese language learners
Curriculum
AC9LC8EC05

About this resource

AI translation can give you a usable answer very quickly. The harder question is whether that answer fits the situation. In Chinese, the same expression can work differently depending on who is speaking, who they are speaking to, and what they are trying to do.

Start with one phrase

What does 不好意思 mean?

An AI tool might give you several reasonable options: “sorry”, “excuse me”, “I feel embarrassed”, or “sorry to bother you”. None of those is automatically the right choice.

不好意思，我迟到了。

“Sorry, I’m late.”

不好意思，请问洗手间在哪里？

“Excuse me, where is the bathroom?”

真不好意思，又麻烦你了。

“I’m sorry to bother you again.”

The Chinese expression stays the same, but the English changes. Using AI for translation still requires judgement.

Before you use the translation

Ask three questions

01

What is happening?

Who is speaking? Who are they speaking to? What are they trying to do: apologise, get someone’s attention, soften a request, or show consideration?

02

What are the other options?

Do not stop at the first answer. Ask for alternatives, then compare them with your class notes, a dictionary, an example you have already seen, or your teacher. A translation can be grammatical and still sound odd in that situation.

03

What is hard to carry across?

Some meaning sits in tone, politeness, relationships and cultural habits. AI can suggest wording, but you still need to decide what the speaker is doing in the conversation and how that would normally be expressed in English.

Before you paste anything

Keep private information out of AI tools

Do not paste your full name, address, school login details, private messages, photographs, or another student’s work into an AI tool. If you are unsure whether something is safe to upload, ask your teacher first.

Your turn

Which translation would you use?

Chinese

老师，辛苦了。

Situation

You say this to your teacher after a lesson.

AI gives three possible translations:

  • “You’ve worked hard, teacher.”
  • “Thanks for all your hard work.”
  • “You must be tired.”

Which one would you use? Would you change any of them? Explain what the speaker is trying to communicate.
