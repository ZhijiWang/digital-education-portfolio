Can AI Translate This?

A 5C Check for Year 7–8 Chinese

For
Year 7–8 Chinese language learners
Curriculum
AC9LC8EC05

How to use the 5C Check

AI translation can be useful, but a fluent answer is not always the best answer. Chinese expressions can change meaning depending on who is speaking, the situation and the relationship between speakers. Before you use an AI translation, complete the 5C Check.

The 5C Check

Pause before you use an AI translation

  1. 1

    Context

    What is happening?

    Ask: Who is speaking? To whom? Where? Why? For example, 加油 might appear as “Add oil!” if translated literally. At a sports game, “Come on!”, “Keep going!” or “You can do it!” may communicate the intended meaning more accurately.

  2. 2

    Check

    Is the meaning accurate?

    AI can sound confident even when an answer is inaccurate or awkward. Check important translations against another source, such as class materials, a dictionary, an authentic example or your teacher. Do not trust an answer only because it sounds fluent.

  3. 3

    Culture

    What might have disappeared?

    Some expressions carry information about politeness, relationships or cultural expectations. Consider 辛苦了. “You have worked hard” may reproduce the words but miss what the speaker is doing socially. Ask: What does the speaker want the other person to understand or feel?

  4. 4

    Confidentiality

    Should I give this information to AI?

    Stop before entering personal or private information. Do not upload names, addresses, private conversations, photographs, school login details or another student’s work unless your teacher has approved the tool and activity.

  5. 5

    Create

    You make the final decision

    AI gives you an option. You are responsible for the final meaning. Choose KEEP if the translation works, CHANGE if part needs revision, or REJECT if it misses the intended meaning. Be ready to explain your choice.

Worked example

One phrase, several possibilities

加油 → “Add oil!”

Depending on the situation, a contextual translation could be:

  • “Come on!”
  • “Keep going!”
  • “You can do it!”
